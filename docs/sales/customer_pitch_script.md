# Customer Stakeholder Pitch — Spoken Script

**Audience:** Restaurant and supplier prospects (sales pitch)  
**Duration:** ~7–8 minutes at conversational pace (~1,050 words)  
**Format:** Choose one opener, then read the shared core through to the close.

**How to use this script:** Pick the opener that matches who is in the room. The shared core works for mixed audiences — restaurant owners, supplier owners, and their ops managers. Stage directions are in parentheses.

---

## **[RESTAURANT OPENER]** — ~30 seconds

If you run a restaurant, you already know the drill.

You work with five, ten, maybe fifteen suppliers — and every one of them is on a different channel. You place orders on WhatsApp. You track inventory in your head, or in a spreadsheet someone updates when they have time. And at the end of the month, you reconcile invoices hoping nothing slipped through.

(pause)

That is not a workflow problem. That is a _system_ problem. And it gets worse every time you add a supplier or open a second location.

---

## **[SUPPLIER OPENER]** — ~30 seconds

If you are a distributor, your phone is your order desk.

Restaurants call you. They WhatsApp you. They email you. And your team re-types everything into a notebook or Excel before anyone can pick, pack, and deliver.

(pause)

One missed message is a missed sale. One wrong quantity is a damaged relationship. And at the end of the month, you are chasing payments instead of growing your customer base.

That is the reality for most distributors in this market — and it does not scale.

---

## **SHARED CORE**

### The problem — both sides — ~45 seconds | starts ~0:30

Restaurants and suppliers are stuck in the same broken loop.

On the restaurant side: there is no single view of what was ordered, what arrived, and what is owed. Inventory, orders, and supplier conversations live in different places. When you add a location, the manual work multiplies — it does not get simpler.

On the supplier side: orders arrive through every channel imaginable, and consolidating them for fulfillment is error-prone. You have limited visibility into which customers order what and how often. Invoicing and collections take time away from the work that actually grows the business.

The cost of calling this "good enough" is real — wasted hours every week, stockouts from slow reordering, disputes when something does not match, and relationships that fray when orders or invoices are wrong.

We are not here to sell you digital transformation. We are here because both sides deserve one place to order, receive, communicate, and get paid.

---

### What Supplify is — ~60 seconds | starts ~1:15

Supplify is the operating system for restaurant–supplier relationships.

(pause — let that land)

One platform. Not five apps and a notebook.

We connect restaurants and their suppliers in a single, role-based system — so you order, fulfill, receive, invoice, and communicate in one place, instead of chasing each other across WhatsApp, phone calls, and spreadsheets.

This is not a concept on a slide. The platform is built — ordering, fulfillment with live GPS tracking, receiving with quality capture, invoicing, disputes, bilingual catalogs, multi-branch inventory, and real-time chat tied to your orders. We are launching commercially now and onboarding our founding cohort personally.

What we are asking is not "trust us, we will build it." It is "try it — and tell us what your day actually looks like."

---

### A day in the life — order to pay — ~90 seconds | starts ~2:15

Let me walk you through what a normal day looks like on Supplify.

**(emphasis)** Morning. Your restaurant manager opens a saved quick list — the items you reorder every week — builds a cart from your linked supplier catalogs, and places the order in one click. No WhatsApp thread. No phone tag. The supplier sees it immediately on their fulfillment board.

Midday. The supplier confirms the order, dispatches a driver, and you can track delivery live on a map — not "call me when you are close."

At delivery. The driver captures proof of delivery. Your team records what arrived, with photos if something does not look right. Fewer arguments later, because the record is already there.

After delivery. The invoice links directly to the order and the receiving record. Both sides see payment status. And if anyone has a question — "did we agree on this price?" or "where is this delivery?" — the chat lives right on the order. No digging through message history.

(pause)

For restaurants, that means less time reconciling and more time running the floor.

For suppliers, it means fewer missed or duplicate orders, clearer prioritization of your customers, and invoicing that does not live in a separate spreadsheet.

### Why Supplify — ~60 seconds | starts ~3:45

You might ask: why not just keep using WhatsApp? Or why not use one of the bigger platforms already in the market?

Fair questions.

WhatsApp is a channel. It is not a system. It does not track inventory, link invoices to orders, dispatch drivers with GPS, or give you an audit trail when something goes wrong.

As for other platforms — tools like Supy are built for larger multi-branch groups, typically starting around two hundred fifty dollars a month with a sales conversation. POS systems like Foodics are excellent at the register, but procurement is not their core — restaurants still coordinate suppliers separately.

Supplify sits in a different place. We are a **two-sided** platform — restaurants and suppliers on the same system — with the operational depth a growing chain needs, priced so a single independent restaurant can actually afford it. Plans start at forty-nine dollars a month.

We built this for Lebanon first — Arabic and English catalogs, local relationships, local support. And we do not take a commission on your order value. You pay a flat subscription. Predictable, month after month.

(pause)

### Close — ~30 seconds | starts ~5:45

Here is what I would suggest as a next step.

Start a free thirty-day trial — or, if you prefer, book a twenty-minute walkthrough using your actual supplier list or catalog. We will onboard you personally. You are not signing up for software and hoping someone answers the phone. You are joining a founding cohort, and your feedback shapes what we build next.

(pause)

The question is not whether your operation can afford to try this. It is whether you can afford another year of ordering, delivering, and invoicing across five different channels.

Let us fix that together.

## What this pitch deliberately omits

- Investor metrics (LTV, CAC, break-even, fundraising)
- Lebanon macro or political risk — not relevant to a software buying decision
- Global market size / CAGR statistics — category validation for investors, not operators
- Developer API webhooks / catalog-only Scale items — do not sell what is not shipped for that account
